About Kendrick

Born in 1987, the height of the Crack epidemic in Compton, Kendrick Lamar grew up in a neighborhood marked by violence and poverty. Despite these challenges, he found solace in music and began rapping at a young age. His early experiences shaped his perspective and influenced his lyrical content, which often addresses the harsh realities of urban life. He could have easily became a product of his environment and internalize the negative influences, but instead he pushed through and creates music that expresses and appreciates his roots, while simultaneously calling out and denouncing the systemic and insiduous issues that plagues his community. He already had a great underground following before breaking into the mainstream with his Major Label debut album "good kid, m.A.A.d city." Once he released this album, he gained widespread recognition and critical acclaim. Your'e probably already familiar with a few songs like "HUMBLE","B*tch Don't Kill My Vibe", "Alright", and even more recently "Not Like Us". But let me put you on to some of my favorite songs that really speak to me the most, and hopefully inspire you to take your own journey into his discography. Take a moment, sit down, and listen intently.

My Favorite Songs

Ignorance Is Bliss

Mixtape: Overly Dedicated (2010)

This track is titled after a popular idiom which translates to “what you don’t know can’t hurt you.” Kendrick raps the first verse without taking a single breath. As raunchy as it may sound, he really taps into the mental of the average crook. He breaks down in first person how an individual can commit henious acts and have no qualms with it, because of blissful ignorance. This particular song is important to Kendrick's career, as this is the first track he heard that sparked Dr. Dre's interest working with Kendrick.

A.D.H.D.

Album: Section.80 (2011)

Kendrick Lamar demonstrates a robust knowledge of drug culture and neurotransmitters on “A.D.H.D.” He mentions such topics as tolerance, endorphins, and cold water extraction. This knowledge does not come without some regret, as the song is largely a commentary on the prevalence of drug abuse in not just his generation, but the similarities and after effects of the crack epidemic in the 80s. This song was also featured on the very popular game released in 2012, Grand Theft Auto.

Sing About Me, I'm Dying of Thirst

Album: good kid, m.A.A.d city (2012)

Kendrick Lamar’s two-part masterpiece, “Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst,” depicts life in an urban ghetto. From beginning to end, Lamar breaks down the stories that pulled him away from gang culture, while also speaking on his faith and the spiritual rebirth that provided him with an escape from the streets. He takes the perspective of two different people before rapping from his own perspective to discuss his trials and tribulations. This track is for sure a tearjerker.

The Blacker the Berry

Album: To Pimp A Butterfly (2015)

Even though this track is particularly dark and politically charged, "The Blacker the Berry" received rave reviews from contemporary music critics for its cinematic production, social commentary, and confrontational lyrics. Kendrick celebrates his African American heritage while simultaneously confronting hatred and hypocrisy, but importantly not forgetting to explore possible personal complicity.

FEAR.

Album: DAMN. (2017)

On “FEAR.,” Kendrick explores three instances of true terror at the ages 7, 17, and 27. The first verse explores the fear he experienced growing up with a strict mother. The second verse explores the life of a teenage Kendrick and the fear of dying at a young age in the ghetto. Lastly in the third and fourth verse, he explores the fear of being famous and losing it all.

luther

Album: GNX (2024)

In Kendrick’s verses, he raps about envisioning a world where pain and conflict are eradicated, replaced by light and empowerment for his lover, and how she keeps him grounded, level-headed, and inspired. SZA’s ethereal voice provides a soulful counterpart, infusing the track with raw vulnerability and a deep yearning for connection and growth with her partner.
